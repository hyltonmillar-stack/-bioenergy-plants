Generation mix, 2009 to 2026

Annual energy (TWh) by source, summed from NESO's half-hourly series. Coal fell from 99 TWh in 2009 to zero after Ratcliffe-on-Soar closed on 30 September 2024. Wind (transmission plus embedded) has been the largest single source since 2024, ahead of gas. Imports doubled between 2022 and 2024.

Source: NESO Historic GB Generation Mix, half-hourly MW × 0.5 h. 2026 = 1 January to 20 October. NESO series covers GB transmission-metered generation plus embedded wind and solar estimates; it excludes most other distribution-connected generation and differs from DESNZ DUKES (UK, all generators) — see the DESNZ section.

All connected offshore wind farms (TEC register)

Multi-phase farms are combined (for example Hornsea One = 1A + 1B + 1C). Smaller farms connected to distribution networks are not on the TEC register and are not shown. Sources: NESO TEC Register (16 Oct 2026) and Interconnector Register; NESO Historic Demand Data 2025 (interconnector and Scottish transfer flows). Base map: Natural Earth via world-atlas.

Largest connected stations (TEC, MW)

TEC is a contracted export right, not nameplate output. Drax holds 3,906 MW of TEC, including rights from its former coal units; its four biomass units total about 2.6 GW. Dogger Bank A and B show full TEC although construction was still being completed in 2026.

Flows

Interconnectors and the Scotland–England boundary

GB had ten operating interconnectors in 2026 with about 10.7 GW of import capacity (plus the 74 MW Isle of Man link) on the NESO Interconnector Register. In 2025 GB imported 43.9 TWh gross and exported 14.8 TWh, a net import of 29.1 TWh on NESO's flow data (DESNZ DUKES: 29.7 TWh, UK basis). Flows to the island of Ireland are consistently net exports; flows from France and Norway are consistently net imports.
